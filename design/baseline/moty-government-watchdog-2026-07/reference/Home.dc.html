HOW THIS STAYED LEGAL — AND STILL SLIPPED BY
Wyoming's Public Meetings Act (W.S. §16-4-404) requires a meeting agenda to be posted in advance, but it does not require a fresh public notice when a supporting packet is amended. So the $150 fee language added to page 3 on Jul 5 — two days before the vote, after most residents had already read version 1 — went out with no announcement. No law was broken. The change simply never had to be surfaced, which is exactly the gap we flag.

FEATURED STORY
TOWN
Six-Month Building Pause Goes to a Vote
Resolution 2024-033 would pause building permits and annexation petitions in six zoning districts while a sewer-capacity study lands. Single-family homes are exempt.
Updated July 5, 2026 ✓ 4 sources verified ▲ packet changed after posting
Alpine, Wyoming — the Snake River corridor drives the growth behind the pause.
PLAIN-ENGLISH SUMMARY AI
The town wants a six-month pause on most new building and annexation while it checks whether the sewer can keep up. Existing homes and single-family builds continue as normal.
WHY IT MATTERS
The town engineer's study says full build-out would use 92% of worst-day sewer capacity. A pause buys time — but stalls projects like Alpine Flats buildings 3 & 4.
NEXT ACTION
Public comment and a possible vote at the Tuesday 6:00 PM council meeting, Town Hall. Anyone may speak or submit written comment.
